Problem definition02 / 12

Money moves in. Momentum doesn't.

A first-time investor completes KYC, deposits money — and a large share never place a first trade. This is not a Zerodha-specific quirk; it's a structural pattern across Indian retail investing.

30–40%
of India's demat accounts show any transaction in a 12-month window — the rest sit dormant.1
41.7%
of first-time investors who do eventually invest take about a month to place their first trade.2
9.5%
of Indian households actually invest in market products, vs. 63% who are already aware of them.3

The gap isn't awareness or access — both are effectively solved. It's what happens in the seconds after money lands with nowhere obvious to go.

Sources: 1Choice India, dormant demat account analysis · 2Zerodha Kite onboarding teardown (S. Baranwal) · 3Acumen Group, Indian stock market investor participation study

Field research03 / 12

What the app actually shows on day one

Walked a live Kite account rather than assume the flow. Three screens tell most of the story.

Watchlist — default home
A raw ticker grid. No CTA, no "getting started," nothing beginner-facing — the first thing seen after money lands.
Login gate — risk disclosure
SEBI-mandated: "9 out of 10 F&O traders lost money." Necessary for suitability — but often the first substantive message a beginner reads.
Orders → SIPs
The lowest-stakes first action (a SIP) sits several taps deep, behind vocabulary a beginner hasn't learned yet.
Same pattern, desktop web

The Kite dashboard's only real CTA for an empty account is "Start investing" — a good instinct. But it drops straight into a raw instrument search. Searching the natural phrase a beginner would actually type, "mutual fund," returns unexplained exchange codes — GOLDBETA, SBISENSEX, TSIFDG, NCAPBULADD — with no curation, no plain-English label, no "start here." The guidance ends exactly where the user needed it most.

Diagnosis04 / 12

Six reasons the freeze happens

Ranked by where they occur — the freeze starts earlier than "after deposit."

01
The freeze starts during onboarding itself
Walked the signup flow: right after PAN entry, step 2 of 5 asks "what products would you like to start with?" — Equity & Mutual Funds / F&O / Commodity toggles, plus a dense terms block — before the user has seen the app or been told what any of these mean.
02
No default next step
Zerodha deliberately never tells users what to buy — core to the trust brand. But a first-timer needs some scaffolding, and today gets none.
03
Decision paralysis + fear of the wrong move
Real complaints converge on one line: "I put money in, now what do I even buy?" Most arrived on social proof, not a thesis.
04
Education lives outside the decision
Varsity is excellent, but it's a separate app — learning happens as homework, not as help at the moment of choice.
05
Product surface mismatch
A beginner's natural first move (small SIP) is buried inside a trading terminal built for an entirely different persona.
06
No re-engagement loop
Once a user bounces, nothing brings them back — existing nudges are compliance-driven, not activation-driven.
Stakes05 / 12

Why this is worth solving deliberately

Business

  • Dormant accounts generate zero brokerage/AUM against real, already-sunk KYC and compliance cost.
  • Live churn risk — a frustrated first-timer doesn't quit investing, they retry on a competitor with more hand-holding.
  • Depresses reported active-client figures that matter for market-share and investor-confidence optics.

Customer

  • A failed first attempt can sour someone on investing entirely — a financial-wellbeing cost, not just a funnel metric.
  • Many arrived with genuine intent (a bonus, a first salary) — the freeze wastes real motivation.
  • Reinforces the exact financial-literacy gap the broader ecosystem is trying to close.
AI evaluation06 / 12

Is AI the right lens? The explicit call.

AI can be a catalyst here — it's very difficult for it to be the whole solution. This is roughly 70% UX, content, and product structure, and 30% narrowly-scoped AI.

UX / Content / Structure — 70%
Bounded AI — 30%
Regulatory ceiling

Zerodha is a broker, not a registered adviser for retail stock-picking. AI that recommends what to buy is a compliance risk, not a feature.

The bottleneck is structural

Users aren't stuck for lack of someone to ask — they're stuck for lack of a visible, low-risk default action. A chatbot doesn't fix a missing on-ramp.

Trust needs proof, not explanation

Fear of "getting it wrong" is solved by shrinking the stakes of the first action, not by a Q&A interface that assumes the user knows what to ask.

Where AI does earn a role, it accelerates a UX fix that already works — it can't manufacture the missing on-ramp by itself. That's the "catalyst, not solution" distinction.

AI evaluation07 / 12

Where AI earns its place — tightly bounded

Only after the deterministic fix ships. Each use has a hard-coded line it must never cross.

UseWhat it doesWhat it must never do
Content sequencingPersonalizes which Varsity module, and in what order, is surfaced from a short intent quiz.Recommend a specific stock or fund.
Nudge intelligencePropensity model: which dormant user gets which nudge, on which channel, when.Auto-decide what to invest in on the user's behalf.
In-context explainerAnswers "what does P/E mean," "explain this fund" — strictly educational.Answer "should I buy X" — hard-blocked by design.
Target user08 / 12

Who this is designed for

The motivated-but-unanchored first-timer
22–35, urban/semi-urban. Opened the account off social proof — a friend, a finfluencer, a first salary or bonus. Deposited ₹5k–₹25k. Has directional intent ("I should start investing") but no framework or vocabulary yet. Not the trader persona who came for F&O — that segment self-activates immediately.
Time-poorFears "getting it wrong"Trusts peers, not tipsMF-oriented at heart

Voice of the customer

"Where should I even invest?"
"How should I invest — lump sum, SIP, what?"
"How much is safe to put in as a first try?"
"Should I even trust this app with my money?"
Key assumption

"First-time investor" collapses two different people: first time on Zerodha, and first time investing at all. This design targets the latter — the harder case. Signal: Zerodha's own onboarding already asks trading experience ("New" / "1–5 years" / etc.) at signup but doesn't act on the answer — a true first-timer gets the identical unguided drop as a veteran switching brokers.

Solution, in the product09 / 12

"First ₹500" — a guided on-ramp

Not just a concept — five real screens from the clickable prototype, walking the same path a first-timer would tap through. Fully optional; the self-directed flow is untouched.

▶ Try the live, clickable prototype
1 · Start Here card
Appears once, right after deposit. Dismissible — the self-directed flow stays untouched.
2 · Intent, multi-select
Routing only — motivations aren't exclusive, and no security is ever named here.
3 · One tiny first action
₹500 index-fund SIP — small, reversible, framed as what similar first-timers do.
4 · A form, not a decision
The hard thinking already happened, in a lower-stakes place.
5 · Close the loop
Confirms, then teases one small lesson — momentum without pressure.

Also in the flow (not pictured): a "Talk to someone" option at every step for anyone who'd rather not self-serve; short swipeable lesson cards instead of a link out to Varsity; Day 1/3/7 re-engagement nudges; a bounded AI helper that explains but never recommends; and a proactive human callback for high-deposit users who stay inactive past 14 days.

Trade-offs & risks10 / 12

What we're deliberately trading against

Every major dimension the assignment flags — walked through explicitly, not assumed away.

DimensionTrade-off / riskHow we handle it
ComplexityMulti-path routing (self-directed vs. guided vs. lessons) adds real branching logic to build and maintain.Ship the single deterministic path first (month 1–3); other branches are additive, not blocking.
CostPropensity modeling, AI search, and AI calling all need real infrastructure and compliance investment.AI spend is sequenced to month 3–6, scoped first and piloted small, after the free UX fix has already proven the lift.
User trustAny default nudge risks diluting "we never push products" — the core trust brand.Every default is framed as informational, never a recommendation, and fully skippable.
Product suitabilityA single ₹500 index-SIP default may not fit every income or risk profile.Default is deliberately generic and low-stakes; self-directed paths stay fully open.
SafetyAI-generated text near financial decisions is a mis-selling risk if it drifts into advice.Hard-coded refusal pattern, templated/compliance-reviewed copy, no free generation near security names.
ScalabilityRules-based nudges don't get smarter as the dormant cohort grows into millions.Propensity model is built once volume justifies it — not before, per the roadmap.
Speed to shipThe full roadmap spans six months; leadership may want proof sooner.The deterministic MVP ships in month 2 — the activation lift is measurable long before AI enters.
Success measurement11 / 12

How we'll know it worked

Goal: increase activation % — the share of first-time investors who actually invest — not just accounts opened.

Mother metric
% of first-time users who invest at least once within 30 days of first deposit — "Activation Rate."

Leading indicators

  • TAT: median time from deposit to first investment
  • Share of first-time investors vs. all active investors on the platform
  • Guided-path engagement vs. skip-rate, and conversion for each
  • 90-day second-transaction rate — activation that sticks, not one-and-done

Guardrail metrics

  • Avg. investment amount of first-time investors — should stay small/starter-sized, not creep up
  • Support tickets citing "felt pushed" / mis-selling concern
  • Any SEBI / advisory-boundary escalation — must stay zero
  • 30-day cancellation rate on guided SIPs
  • Churn rate, guided cohort vs. control
Execution12 / 12

Six-month roadmap

AI design starts month 3, first bounded pilots ship month 4 — after the deterministic fix is proven, never as a substitute for it.

Month 1
Foundation
Instrumentation, research & CX journey
Baseline activation measurement, deposit→first-trade event tracking, 10–15 first-timer interviews, compliance review of guided-path language — synthesized into a documented CX journey map (VoC, onboarding walkthrough, desktop-search findings) that becomes the reference for everything shipped after.
No AI
Month 2
MVP
Ship the deterministic on-ramp
Start Here card, multi-select intent router, static paths incl. human-callback request, ₹500 index-SIP default — A/B on new-signup cohort.
No AI
Month 3
Iterate
Measure, fix & scope the AI pilots
Analyze 30-day activation lift and time-to-first-investment; fix drop-off points; expand paths using real routing data. In parallel: design + compliance-scope two bounded AI experiments — natural-language search/discovery, and an outbound AI-calling script for dormant users.
No AI
Month 4
Re-engagement
Bring back the dormant + AI search pilot
Rules-based Day 1/3/7 nudges across push/email/WhatsApp, frequency capping, opt-out. Pilot AI-assisted search: a generic query like "mutual fund" returns a curated, plain-English shortlist instead of raw ticker codes — zero personalized recommendation, purely a discovery aid.
Bounded AI
Month 5
Pilot
Nudge AI + AI-calling pilot
Propensity model for nudge timing/channel; bounded in-context explainer with hard guardrails, compliance sign-off. Pilot outbound AI calling on a small dormant cohort — scripted, compliance-reviewed, hands off to a human for anything off-script, never recommends a security.
Bounded AI
Month 6
Scale
Institutionalize
Roll out whichever AI experiments proved lift — search discovery, propensity-driven nudges, AI calling; Activation Rate becomes a tracked company metric with a guardrail dashboard.
